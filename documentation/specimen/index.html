The system

One circle

Capitals fill a circle 868 units across, centred 234 units above the baseline. Strokes that reach the edge are trimmed by it, so outer terminals are curved cuts lying on that circle. Figures sit on a smaller concentric circle and are all exactly 788 units tall.

The x-circle

Lowercase is built on a concentric circle of half the radius, giving an x-height of 468. Ascenders and descenders run out to the outer circle. Capitals share the same centre, so they reach 200 units below the baseline.

Arcs first

A stroke is an arc wherever the letter survives it. The sides of H, M, N, U and W and the bars of E, F, L, T and Z run along the circle itself. Stems of B, D, E, F, K, L, P and R are cut from a circle of radius 1,200. Diagonals are bowed, and A's crossbar is swung from its apex. Straight lines remain for vertical axes, crossbars and the math signs. Every stroke is 68 units wide.

Accents

Accents are built from arcs and lines too, drawn slightly lighter than the letters. On lowercase they sit inside the circle. A capital already fills its circle, so its accents sit just outside it, as do accents on ascenders such as ĺ. In Ring and Coin, an accented capital is scaled down until the whole glyph fits inside the ring.

Monograms

Each initial is drawn in its own circle, and every circle in a monogram has the same radius. Two letters form a vesica piscis, each centre lying on the other circle. Three letters form a chain, each circle passing through its neighbour's centre. The outer ring is the font's own circle, so strokes that reach it fuse into the frame. The monogram is built from overlay glyphs placed by contextual alternates, so it needs no special software. In Word, turn on contextual alternates or standard ligatures. Coin has no monograms, because separate overlay glyphs cannot knock letters out of a disc.

Spacing

Regular is proportional with 48-unit side bearings and 3,696 kern pairs measured from glyph profiles. Ring and Coin sit on a 932-unit monospaced cell.
